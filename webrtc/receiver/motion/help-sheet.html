Not a medical device. Movement detection can miss movement or raise false alerts. It is a helpful cue, not a substitute for watching your baby.

What it does

Movement detection tells you when your baby starts moving, for example when waking up. It follows points on the body (shoulders, hips, arms, head) seen by the Camera and uses three detectors. Any one of them can mark your baby as moving:

  • Quick motion: sudden movements such as a kick or an arm flung out.
  • Sustained motion: gentle movement that keeps going, such as squirming.
  • Drift: your baby has slowly shifted away from where they were lying still.

The video is analysed on this device. Nothing is uploaded.

Set up (about 1 minute)

  1. Place the Camera so your baby’s whole body is in view, ideally from above or the side of the cot.
  2. Wait until your baby is calm and still.
  3. Calibrate: open Settings and press Start under Calibration. Keep everything still until the timer ends (15 seconds by default). The sensitivity is then tuned to your room and Camera automatically.
  4. Leave all three detectors on and keep Torso-relative baseline ticked. Adjust settings only if you get false alerts or missed movement (see Fix a problem).

Calibrate again whenever you move the Camera, change the lighting a lot, or move the cot.

Reading the screen

STILL / MOVING The current state, shown in the settings window and on the video. A Movement alert (with a sound if enabled in Settings) starts when your baby starts moving, and ends once they settle.
Bars on the video One per detector, showing its current level. When a bar passes its ON level, your baby counts as moving; it settles once all are back below their OFF levels.
Body overlay The body points being tracked. If they don’t appear, movement can’t be detected.

Fix a problem

Alerts with no real movement Re-calibrate while the scene is calm. Keep Torso-relative baseline on and increase Baseline τ. If one detector causes it, raise its ON level slightly.
Missed movement Re-calibrate, then lower the ON levels a little. Make sure the whole body is in view and the body points appear.
Alerts when the Camera is bumped Keep Torso-relative baseline on, put the Camera on a steady surface, and re-calibrate.
Bars stay at 0 No body points are arriving. Check the Camera is streaming and its body tracking (pose) is on. Lower Visibility min / Presence min slightly in dim light.
Calibration “Start” is greyed out It only works once body points are being received. Check the video and the Camera’s pose setting, then try again.
How it works

Raw movement. For each video frame, the average distance each body point moved since the last frame, divided by the body’s size (torso length), so it doesn’t matter how close the Camera is. Only points that pass Visibility min and Presence min are used.

Quick motion (EMA). A running average that follows the raw movement at speed EMA α: ema = ema + α × (raw − ema). Moving when it reaches EMA ON; still again below EMA OFF.

Sustained motion (Integrator). Adds up movement over time and lets it fade with time constant INT τ, so gentle but continuous movement builds up. Moving when it reaches INT ON; still below INT OFF.

Drift. Compares the current position of the body points with a “still” reference that updates slowly (Baseline τ) while your baby is still. Moving when the difference reaches DRIFT ON; still below DRIFT OFF.

Calibration measures raw movement in a calm scene, then sets each ON level well above that noise (several times the typical value, and above the 95th percentile). Each OFF level sits below its ON level, and a short hold stops the state flickering between moving and still.
